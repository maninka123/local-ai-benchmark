You are an independent investigator assigned to examine a fictional laboratory security incident.

You have been provided with evidence including images, floor plans, witness statements, access records and communications.

Your task is to reconstruct the most likely sequence of events and reach a defensible conclusion.

Do not assume that any witness statement is automatically accurate.

Treat every piece of evidence critically.

**A. Evidence examination**

Inspect all supplied evidence.

For each item:

- Describe relevant observations.
- Identify important details.
- Record timestamps where available.
- Distinguish direct observations from interpretations.
- Note uncertainty or ambiguity.

**B. Timeline reconstruction**

Create a chronological timeline.

Identify:

- Confirmed events
- Probable events
- Contradictory timestamps
- Missing intervals
- Unexplained movements

Explain any assumptions used.

**C. Suspect analysis**

Evaluate each suspect using the available evidence.

Consider:

- Opportunity
- Access
- Timeline consistency
- Contradictions
- Supporting evidence
- Exculpatory evidence
- Uncertainty

Do not treat motive alone as proof.

**D. Multimodal reasoning**

Use visual evidence together with documents and logs.

Interpret:

- Images
- Spatial layouts
- Visible objects
- Movement paths
- Relevant timestamps

Connect findings across evidence sources.

Do not invent details that are not visible or documented.

**E. Final conclusion**

Explain:

- What most likely happened.
- Which observations support the conclusion.
- Which alternative explanations remain plausible.
- Whether a responsible individual can be identified.
- What additional evidence would resolve remaining uncertainty.

If the evidence does not support a definitive conclusion, explicitly state that.

**F. Final report**

Produce a structured investigation report containing:

1. Executive summary
2. Evidence inventory
3. Timeline
4. Suspect analysis
5. Key deductions
6. Contradictions
7. Final conclusion
8. Confidence and limitations
9. Recommended next investigative steps

Use exhibit IDs to support important findings.

Do not fabricate evidence or claim to have observed details that are unavailable.

**G. Output requirements**

Create:

```text
output/
├── investigation_report.md
├── timeline.json
├── evidence_analysis.json
└── conclusions.json
```

Structured JSON outputs must reference the relevant exhibit IDs.
