
# Investigation Report: Laboratory Prototype Theft

## 1. Executive Summary
Between 18:00 and 18:18, a laboratory prototype was removed from the LAB. Visual evidence (V04) shows a person in a teal work coat carrying the prototype at the EAST EXIT at 18:14. Access logs (D01) show Nora Vale's credentials were used to enter the East Corridor at 18:10 and exit the East Exit at 18:14. While the timing and coat color strongly suggest Nora Vale, the use of the service corridor blind spot (V10) and the lack of biometric verification mean the identity of the person using the badge remains unconfirmed.

- **V01 (Floor Plan):** Shows the layout, including the unmonitored SERVICE CORRIDOR link to the LAB.
- **V02 (Lab Camera 18:00):** Confirms prototype presence.
- **V03 (East Corridor Camera 18:10):** Shows a person in a teal coat.
- **V04 (East Exit Camera 18:14):** Shows a person in a teal coat with the prototype.
- **V05 (Lab Camera 18:18):** Confirms prototype absence.
- **V06 (Cafeteria Camera 18:12):** Shows a person in a blue coat (Theo Park).
- **V07 (Reception Camera 18:13):** Shows a person in a red coat (Ada Finch).

- **V10 (Service Camera):** Confirms a blind spot in the SERVICE CORRIDOR.
- **D01 (Access Logs):** Records badge usage for Nora Vale, Theo Park, and Ada Finch.
- **D02 (Witness Statements):** Unverified statements from staff members.
- **D03 (Internal Messages):** Estaminishes motive for Eli Stone and tasking for Maya Reed.

## 4. Suspect Analysis
- **Nora Vale:** **Primary Suspect.** Her badge was used during the exact window of the theft, and a person in her assigned teal coat was seen with the prototype at the exit. However, badge usage does not prove identity.
- **Eli Stone:** **Person of Interest.** Established motive (D03). While no badge logs place him in the area, the service corridor blind spot (V10) provides an unmonitored path to the lab.
- **Maya Reed:** **Cleared.** Observed at the loading bay at 18:11 (V08) performing a documented task.
- **Theo Park:** **Cleared.** Observed in the cafeteria at 18:12 (V06).
- **Ada Finch:** **Cleared.** Observed at reception at 18:13 (V07).

## 5. Key Deductions
- The prototype was moved from the LAB to the EAST EXIT between 18:00 and 18:14.
- The person in the teal coat is the most likely carrier based on visual evidence (V04).
- The service corridor (V10) represents a significant security gap that could allow unlogged movement.

## 6. Contradictions
- Nora Vale's statement that she "only visited the east corridor and left" is contradicted by the visual evidence (V04) showing the prototype in the possession of a person in her coat color at the exit.

## 7. Final Conclusion
While the evidence strongly points to the individual using Nora Vale's credentials (the person in the teal coat), a definitive identification is impossible due to the lack of biometric data and the existence of a camera blind spot. The most likely scenario is that the person in the teal coat removed the prototype, but the possibility of an unlogged entry via the service corridor cannot be ruled out.

## 8. Confidence and Limitations
- **Confidence:** Moderate (65%). The visual and log correlation is strong, but the identity/badge distinction and the blind spot introduce significant uncertainty.
- **Limitations:** Access logs only track badges, not people. The service corridor blind spot (V10) means the movement of other individuals (like Eli Stone) cannot be fully ruled out.

## 9. Recommended Next Investigative Steps
- Perform biometric verification of the person in the teal coat from footage.
- Review all footage from the loading bay and service corridor periphery.
- Inspect the personal belongings of all staff members with access to the lab.
