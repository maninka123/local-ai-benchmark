Investigation Report: Case 03 — Missing Laboratory Prototype

1. Executive Summary

A laboratory prototype was last confirmed visible in the LAB at 18:00 (V02) and discovered missing at 18:18 (V05). The service corridor provides an unmonitored link to the LAB and is a documented camera blind spot (V10). Two individuals entered via the SERVICE DOOR at 18:14 per badge log (D01), with V04 showing two figures.

The prototype's removal window is between 18:00 and 18:18. No visual evidence confirms any individual's identity beyond coat color, which security explicitly states reflects shift assignments, not biometric identity (D03). Badge logs record credentials, not persons.

The most plausible scenario involves coordinated removal of the prototype through the service corridor blind spot by two individuals entering at 18:14. However, the evidence does not definitively identify who removed the prototype or whether the removal was authorized.

5. Key Deductions

  1. The prototype was removed between 18:00 and 18:18. This is the only window where the prototype transitions from present (V02) to absent (V05).

  2. Two individuals entered via SERVICE DOOR at 18:14. Badge log confirms two entries; V04 shows two figures. The service corridor provides unmonitored access to the LAB (V01, V10).

  3. Eli Stone's statement is contradicted by his badge entry. He claims not to have approached the lab after 18:00, but his SERVICE DOOR entry at 18:14 provides access to the unmonitored link to the LAB.

  4. Maya Reed's statement is incomplete. She accounts for her presence at the loading bay at 18:11 but does not explain her SERVICE DOOR entry at 18:14.

  5. Coat color is evidence of appearance, not identity. Security explicitly states coat descriptions reflect shift assignments (D03). V04 figures are shown in grey schematic, not colored coats.

  6. Badge logs record credentials, not biometric identity. A badge could be used by anyone in possession of it.

6. Contradictions

  1. Eli Stone's statement vs. badge log: Claims no lab approach after 18:00 but has SERVICE DOOR entry at 18:14 (D01).
  2. Maya Reed's statement incompleteness: Accounts for loading bay at 18:11 but not SERVICE DOOR at 18:14 (D01, D02).
  3. Identity uncertainty: Coat colors match shift assignments but do not confirm biometric identity of individuals in V03-V08.
  4. Credential vs. person: Badge entries confirm credential use but not which individual used them (D03).

7. Final Conclusion

The most likely scenario is that the prototype was removed through the unmonitored service corridor by two individuals who entered via the SERVICE DOOR at 18:14. The timing of this entry, combined with the blind spot and the prototype's disappearance window, strongly suggests coordinated removal.

Eli Stone is a person of interest due to:

  • His SERVICE DOOR badge entry at 18:14 (providing access to the lab)
  • His statement contradicting his badge log
  • His earlier message expressing need for the prototype

Maya Reed is a person of interest due to:

  • Her SERVICE DOOR badge entry at 18:14
  • Her incomplete statement regarding her movements
  • Her possession of a cardboard box (potential transport method)

However, the evidence does not definitively identify the perpetrator(s). Key limitations include:

  • Coat color does not confirm biometric identity
  • Badge logs record credentials, not persons
  • The service corridor is a blind spot with no visual evidence
  • Witness statements are unverified and may be incomplete or false
  • No evidence confirms who carried the prototype out

Plausible alternative explanations:

  1. Authorized removal by staff with legitimate access (e.g., for Eli's project)
  2. Unauthorized removal by individuals not in the five-staff list
  3. Earlier removal before 18:00 (less likely given V02 confirmation)
  4. Badge credential misuse by someone other than the badge holder

8. Confidence and Limitations

Confidence level: Low to moderate for scenario reconstruction; very low for individual identification.

Limitations:

  • Visual evidence is schematic, not photographic; coat colors are assigned per shift, not biometric identifiers
  • Badge logs record credential use, not person identity
  • Service corridor is a documented blind spot with no visual coverage
  • Witness statements are unverified and may be incomplete or false
  • No evidence confirms who carried the prototype out of the facility
  • The 18-minute window allows for multiple scenarios

What additional evidence would resolve uncertainty:

  • Service corridor camera footage (currently a blind spot)
  • Biometric verification of badge holders at SERVICE DOOR
  • Box contents verification from V08
  • Authorization records for prototype removal
  • Additional witness statements or surveillance data
