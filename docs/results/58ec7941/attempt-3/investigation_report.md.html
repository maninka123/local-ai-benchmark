Investigation Report: Missing Laboratory Prototype

1. Executive Summary

The laboratory prototype was last confirmed at 18:00 and discovered missing at 18:18. This investigation examined access logs (D01), witness statements (D02), and internal communications (D03). Visual evidence (V01–V10) is unavailable in this text-only version. Based on the written evidence, the investigation concludes that the responsible individual cannot be identified with confidence. While Maya Reed and Eli Stone have access logs near the time of the incident and proximity to the blind spot, the evidence does not establish theft or intent. Badge logs do not confirm identity, and witness claims are unverified.

2. Evidence Inventory

D01: Access Log

  • Observations: Five staff entries logged between 18:10 and 18:14.
  • Limitations: Logs badge credentials, not biometric identity. Does not confirm physical presence or actions.

D02: Witness Statements

  • Observations: Self-reported accounts of location and actions by five staff members.
  • Limitations: Unverified. No corroboration from other sources or visual evidence.

D03: Internal Messages

  • Observations: Three messages. Eli expresses need for the prototype (17:00). Maya requests moving a cardboard box (17:10). Security notes coat descriptions are from assignments and the service corridor is a camera blind spot (17:20).
  • Limitations: No proof of intent to steal. The box request is a request, not an instruction to steal.

V01–V10: Visual Exhibits

  • Observations: Not available in this text-only version.
  • Limitations: Cannot confirm movements, box contents, or visual proximity to the blind spot.

5. Key Deductions

  • Badge vs. Identity: Access logs (D01) indicate credential use, not verified personal identity. A badge entry does not confirm the person's physical presence or actions.
  • Witness Reliability: Witness statements (D02) are self-reported and unverified. They cannot be treated as objective observations.
  • Blind Spot Location: D03 identifies the Service Corridor as the blind spot, not the Service Door. The Service Door is the access point logged in D01.
  • Box Contents: D03 mentions a "cardboard consumables box" requested by Maya. There is no evidence in the written documents that this box contained the prototype or that it was removed.

6. Contradictions

  • Eli Stone: Access log shows entry at 18:14 via SERVICE_DOOR. Witness statement claims he did not approach the lab after 18:00.
  • Maya Reed: Witness statement claims she carried a box at 18:11. No corroborating visual evidence or log entry for box movement exists.
  • Nora Vale: Witness statement claims she only visited the east corridor and left. The proximity of the east corridor to the Service Door/Corridor is not established by the written documents.

7. Final Conclusion

Based on the written evidence (D01–D03), the investigation cannot identify a responsible individual for the missing prototype. The evidence is insufficient to establish theft or intent. While Maya Reed and Eli Stone have access logs near the time of the incident and proximity to the blind spot, the evidence does not confirm their actions or guilt. Badge logs do not confirm identity, and witness claims are unverified. The box mentioned in the messages is not confirmed to contain the prototype or to have been removed.

8. Confidence and Limitations

  • Confidence: Low (10%).
  • Limitations:
    • Visual evidence (V01–V10) is unavailable, preventing confirmation of movements, box contents, or visual proximity to the blind spot.
    • Badge logs (D01) do not confirm identity, only credential use.
    • Witness statements (D02) are unverified and self-reported.
    • The "blind spot" is identified as the Service Corridor (D03), but the access point is the Service Door (D01). The physical route between them is not established.

9. Recommended Next Steps

  • Obtain and review visual evidence (V01–V10) to confirm movements and box contents.
  • Verify identities of badge holders using biometric data or additional surveillance.
  • Interview staff to corroborate witness statements.
  • Inspect the cardboard box mentioned in the messages to determine its contents and location.
