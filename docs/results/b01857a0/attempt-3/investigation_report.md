# Investigation Report: Case_02 - Missing Laboratory Prototype

## 1. Executive Summary
Between 18:00 and 18:18, a laboratory prototype was removed from the Lab. The investigation identifies Eli Stone as the primary suspect. Evidence shows Eli Stone entered the facility via the Service Door at 18:14, a location that provides unmonitored access to the Lab via the Service Corridor (V01). Crucially, Eli Stone's statement claiming he did not approach the lab after 18:00 (D02) is directly contradicted by both badge access logs (D01) and security camera footage (V04), which shows him in an orange coat (matching his assignment) at the Service Door. While Maya Reed's movement with a box (V08) presents a secondary possibility, the direct contradiction and motive associated with Eli Stone make him the most likely individual responsible.

## 4. Suspect Analysis

### Eli Stone (Primary Suspect)
*   **Opportunity**: High. Entered via the Service Door at 18:14, which connects to the Service Corridor—an unmonitored link directly to the Lab (V01).
*   **Access**: Confirmed via badge logs (D01) and camera V04.
*   **Supporting Evidence**: Motive established via message (D03). Presence in the unmonitored corridor at the time of the theft.
*   **Exculpatory Evidence**: None.
*   **Contradictions**: His statement ("I did not approach the lab after 18:00" - D02) is refuted by the badge log (D01) and camera V04.

### Maya Reed
*   **Opportunity**: Moderate. Seen moving a box in the Loading Bay at 18:11 (V08).
*   **Access**: Present in the facility.
*   **Supporting Evidence**: The box could potentially conceal the prototype.
*   **Exculpatory Evidence**: Her movement was part of a documented task (D03).

### Nora Vale, Theo Park, Ada Finch
*   **Opportunity**: Low. Their movements (East Corridor, Cafeteria, Reception) were captured on monitored cameras and do not show direct access to the Lab during the critical window.
*   **Supporting Evidence**: None.
*   **Exculpatory Evidence**: Captured on cameras in non-Lab areas.

## 5. Key Deductions
*   The theft occurred between 18:00 and 18:18.
*   The Service Door/Corridor provides a path to the Lab that bypasses standard monitoring.
*   The discrepancy between Eli Stone's statement and the corrected camera/badge data is the most significant indicator of culpability.

## 6. Contradictions
*   **Eli Stone's Statement vs. Evidence**: Eli claims he did not approach the lab after 18:00 (D02), but badge logs (D01) and camera V04 (corrected to 18:14) place him at the Service Door, which leads to the Lab via an unmonitored path.

## 7. Final Conclusion
Eli Stone is the most likely individual responsible for the removal of the prototype. The combination of motive (D03), opportunity via the unmonitored Service Corridor (V01), and a demonstrably false statement regarding his proximity to the lab (D02 vs D01/V04) provides a strong basis for this conclusion. While Maya Reed's movement with a box (V08) remains a plausible alternative, the evidence against Stone is more compelling.

## 8. Confidence and Limitations
*   **Confidence**: 0.7. The identification of the person in the orange coat relies on coat color, which is an appearance-based proxy, not biometric identity. Furthermore, the unmonitored corridor means the exact path taken cannot be visually confirmed.
*   **Limitations**: The investigation is limited by the lack of continuous surveillance in the Service Corridor and the reliance on unverified witness statements.

## 9. Recommended Next Investigative Steps
*   Review all available footage from the Loading Bay and Service Door for any other unidentified individuals.
*   Conduct a formal interview with Eli Stone regarding the discrepancy in his statement.
*   Inspect the contents of the cardboard box moved by Maya Reed, if still available.
