CASE_03: Missing Laboratory Prototype - Investigation Report

1. Executive Summary

This investigation examines the disappearance of a laboratory prototype between 18:00 and 18:18. Five staff members were present in the facility during this period. The evidence indicates that two individuals entered through the Service Door at 18:14, but badge credentials do not confirm biometric identity and coat colors in visual exhibits may not match shift assignments. The Service Corridor is a blind spot with no camera coverage between 18:14 and 18:18 when the prototype disappeared. While Eli Stone has documented motive (message at 17:00), Maya Reed was observed in Loading Bay at 18:11 and both entered through Service Door, neither can be definitively identified as responsible. The absence of continuous surveillance footage and reliable visual identification prevents a defensible attribution.

Key Finding: The evidence does not support identifying a single responsible individual for the prototype removal with confidence above 35%.

5. Key Deductions

  1. The prototype was removed between 18:14 and 18:18 - V02 shows prototype at 18:00, V05 shows absence at 18:18.

  2. Two individuals entered through Service Door at 18:14 - V04 and D01_access.csv confirm two figures at Service Door entrance.

  3. Service Corridor is a blind spot - V10 explicitly states "BLIND SPOT: SERVICE CORRIDOR"; no camera coverage exists for this area.

  4. Badge credentials do not confirm identity - D03_messages.md notes doors log badge credentials, not biometric identity.

  5. Coat colors in visual exhibits may not match shift assignments - V04 shows grey coats at Service Door, but expected wearers are Eli (orange) and Maya (purple).

  6. No continuous surveillance footage exists - Camera feeds are snapshots, not continuous monitoring.

  7. The removal route is unobserved - No visual evidence shows the prototype being removed or its path from Lab to exit.

7. Final Conclusion

Most Likely Account: The prototype was removed through the Service Corridor blind spot between 18:14 and 18:18 by one or both individuals who entered through the Service Door at 18:14. Visual evidence shows two grey-coated figures at the Service Door entrance (V04), but coat colors do not match expected wearers (Eli=orange, Maya=purple). Badge credentials used at 18:14 do not confirm biometric identity.

Plausible Alternatives:

  • The individuals at Service Door may have swapped coats with shift workers
  • Badge credentials could have been used by someone other than the badge owner
  • A third party with access credentials may have entered through an unlogged route
  • The prototype may have been removed earlier or later than the 18:14-18:18 window

Can Responsibility Be Identified? No. The evidence does not support identifying a single responsible individual with confidence above 35%.

Supported Conclusion: The removal occurred through the Service Corridor blind spot between 18:14 and 18:18, but the specific responsible party cannot be definitively identified from available evidence.

8. Confidence and Limitations

Confidence Level: 35% confidence that removal occurred through Service Corridor between 18:14-18:18; <20% confidence in identifying any specific responsible individual.

Key Limitations:

  • No continuous surveillance of Service Corridor (blind spot)
  • Badge credentials do not confirm biometric identity
  • Coat colors in visual exhibits may not match actual wearers
  • Snapshot-only camera feeds cannot establish continuous whereabouts
  • Missing laboratory-entry footage for the critical interval
  • Visual identification relies on potentially unreliable coat color matching

9. Recommended Next Investigative Steps

  1. Review badge credential logs - Cross-reference with biometric access control if available
  2. Interview shift supervisors - Verify actual coat assignments and any coat swapping
  3. Check security footage from adjacent areas - May capture approach to Service Door
  4. Examine prototype storage logs - Determine if removal was authorized or documented
  5. Review communication records - Identify additional motive or planning discussions
  6. Conduct physical inspection - Check for tool marks, entry points, or other evidence in Lab and Service Corridor
  7. Expand witness interviews - Seek additional personnel who may have observed the incident
  8. Request maintenance logs - Verify if Service Door access was authorized during this period
